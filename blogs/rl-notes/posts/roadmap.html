1. Introduction

This article documents a structured collection of study and research notes on reinforcement learning for robotics. It brings together core learning resources, summaries of important algorithms, and personal reflections developed during the learning process.

  • A curated collection of learning resources for reinforcement learning, including textbooks, courses, and papers.
  • Structured notes and summaries of key RL concepts and algorithms such as PPO, SAC, and hierarchical reinforcement learning.
  • Personal insights and reflections formed during study and research.

Main Objectives

  • Organize high-quality RL learning materials into a clear and structured roadmap.
  • Deepen understanding through summarization and critical reflection.
  • Build a long-term knowledge base for reinforcement learning.

2. Learning Roadmap

2.1 Fundamentals

Mathematical Foundation of Reinforcement Learning

This resource covers reinforcement learning from fundamental concepts to modern algorithms in a mathematically grounded and structured way. It moves from foundational tools such as MDPs, Bellman equations, and dynamic programming to core methods including Monte Carlo, temporal-difference learning, policy gradients, and actor-critic algorithms.

The chapters are strongly connected and are well suited for sequential study, making the book a solid starting point for building systematic understanding.

Note: The focus is mainly on theoretical foundations and classical RL methods, rather than newer deep RL algorithms such as DDPG, TD3, PPO, or SAC.

Book: https://github.com/MathFoundationRL/Book-Mathematical-Foundation-of-Reinforcement-Learning

Video: YouTube Playlist

UC Berkeley CS285: Deep Reinforcement Learning

A comprehensive course covering both the fundamentals of deep reinforcement learning and more advanced research topics. The course includes policy gradients, actor-critic methods, Q-learning, model-based RL, imitation learning, offline RL, exploration, transfer learning, and meta-learning.

Its combination of theoretical material, programming assignments, and a research-oriented final project makes it especially useful for connecting concepts with implementation.

Note: This course assumes prior background in machine learning and basic RL, so it may be demanding for beginners.

Course: https://rail.eecs.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse/

2.2 Deep Reinforcement Learning

Deep reinforcement learning extends classical RL by incorporating deep neural networks as function approximators, allowing agents to learn from high-dimensional observations such as images and continuous state spaces.

  • DQN: Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning
  • DDPG: Continuous control with deep reinforcement learning
  • PPO: Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms
  • TD3: Addressing Function Approximation Error in Actor-Critic Methods
  • SAC: Soft Actor-Critic: Off-Policy Maximum Entropy Deep Reinforcement Learning with a Stochastic Actor

2.3 New Research Topics

Recent RL research has increasingly focused on improving sample efficiency and data utilization, rather than introducing entirely new algorithmic families. Several directions stand out as especially relevant to robotics.

  • Offline Reinforcement Learning. Learning policies from fixed datasets without online interaction, which is especially attractive for safety-critical or expensive robotic systems.

    CQL: Conservative Q-Learning for Offline Reinforcement Learning

    BCQ: Off-Policy Deep Reinforcement Learning without Exploration

    BEAR: Stabilizing Off-Policy Q-Learning via Bootstrapping Error Reduction

  • Model-Based RL and World Models. Learning system dynamics to support planning and improve sample efficiency, often paired with representation learning.

    PlaNet: Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels

    Dreamer: Dream to Control: Learning Behaviors by Latent Imagination

3. RL Tools and Frameworks

This section focuses on tools and libraries that are especially useful for reinforcement learning research and robotics experimentation.

3.1 RL Libraries

  • Stable-Baselines3: Standard implementations of popular RL algorithms such as PPO, SAC, and DQN, widely used for benchmarking and fast prototyping.
  • RLlib: A scalable RL framework supporting distributed training and multi-agent systems.
  • CleanRL: Minimal and transparent implementations that are especially helpful for understanding algorithm details.

3.2 Environments and Benchmarks

  • Gym / Gymnasium: Standardized interface for RL environments.
  • MuJoCo: A widely used simulator for continuous control benchmarks.
  • DeepMind Control Suite: Physics-based control tasks for RL research.
  • Atari Learning Environment: A classic benchmark suite for evaluating RL algorithms.
