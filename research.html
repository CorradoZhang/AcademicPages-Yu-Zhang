Research Overview

My research focuses on reinforcement learning and optimization methods for decision-making in uncertain environments. I am interested in how learning systems can be made robust, adaptive, and computationally efficient for use in autonomous agents and robotics.

Research Project

Human-in-the-Loop Flow Policy Learning for Robotic Manipulation

Timeline: 2025 - Present

Status: In progress

This project develops a human-in-the-loop imitation learning framework for robotic manipulation in the Robomimic simulation environment. The system builds upon Flow Matching policies and improves their behavior through uncertainty-aware intervention, human takeover, and online policy adaptation. When the policy fails or enters an uncertain state, a human operator can repair the problematic trajectory segment through a web-based control interface. Successful recovery demonstrations are then incorporated into online training to improve future autonomous performance.

Research Details

  • Develops a Flow Matching policy for low-dimensional robotic manipulation, generating action sequences from recent robot and object observations.
  • Introduces an intrinsic uncertainty estimator based on the Flow Matching velocity field, using Gaussian mixture modelling to identify inconsistent or multimodal action directions.
  • Implements a budget-constrained human-in-the-loop system that requests human assistance only for selected episodes or high-risk states, while recording the total intervention cost.
  • Provides a browser-based human takeover interface with trajectory playback, multi-view simulation rendering, temporal segment selection, mouse-based Cartesian control, keyboard control, and recovery comparison.
  • Trains a multitask intervention network that predicts both the probability of requiring human assistance and a bounded residual action for correcting the Flow Policy output.
  • Supports iterative trajectory recovery: the operator selects a failure interval, takes control of the robot, and returns control to the Flow Policy from the human-modified state.
  • Performs online imitation learning using a mixture of original Robomimic demonstrations and accepted successful HITL recovery trajectories, reducing catastrophic forgetting during adaptation.
  • Evaluates the method on Robomimic manipulation benchmarks using autonomous success rate, assisted success rate, human-control steps, intervention frequency, uncertainty quality, and post-adaptation performance.

Technologies: Flow Matching · Imitation Learning · Human-in-the-Loop Learning · Uncertainty Estimation · Residual Policy Learning · Online Adaptation · Robomimic · Robosuite · MuJoCo · PyTorch · Web-Based Robot Teleoperation

Modern Replay: Stable and High-Performance Sampling for Deep RL

Timeline: November 2025 - Present

Status: In progress

Current Output: D-SPEAR: Dual-Stream Prioritized Experience Adaptive Replay for Stable Reinforcement Learning in Robotic Manipulation

Modern Replay is a research project focused on improving experience replay mechanisms to achieve both training stability and high performance in deep reinforcement learning. It introduces structured and adaptive sampling strategies that decouple optimization dynamics, reduce gradient variance, and enable more stable and efficient policy learning.

Research Details

  • Proposes a modern replay framework that improves both stability and performance via advanced sampling strategies.
  • Introduces structured sampling mechanisms to balance different learning dynamics during training.
  • Reduces gradient variance, leading to more stable optimization and better convergence behavior.
  • Demonstrates consistent performance gains over standard replay methods on MuJoCo continuous control benchmarks.

Technologies: Deep Reinforcement Learning · Experience Replay · Sampling Optimization · MuJoCo Simulation
