Research Overview

My research focuses on reinforcement learning and optimization methods for decision-making in uncertain environments. I am interested in how learning systems can be made robust, adaptive, and computationally efficient for use in autonomous agents and robotics.

Research Project

Human-in-the-Loop Imitation Learning for Robotic Manipulation

Timeline: 2025 - Present

Status: In progress

This project develops a human-in-the-loop imitation learning framework for robotic manipulation in the Robomimic simulation environment. The system improves learned policies through uncertainty-aware intervention, human takeover, and online adaptation. When the policy fails or enters an uncertain state, a human operator can repair the problematic trajectory segment through a web-based control interface. Successful recovery demonstrations are then incorporated into training to improve future autonomous performance.

Research Details

  • Develops an imitation learning policy for low-dimensional robotic manipulation, generating action sequences from recent robot and object observations.
  • Uses uncertainty estimation and a constrained intervention budget to request human assistance only for selected episodes or high-risk states.
  • Provides a browser-based takeover interface for trajectory playback, failure-segment selection, robot control, and iterative recovery.
  • Performs online adaptation with original Robomimic demonstrations and successful human recovery trajectories, then evaluates autonomous success, intervention cost, and post-adaptation performance.

Technologies: Imitation Learning · Human-in-the-Loop Learning · Uncertainty Estimation · Online Adaptation · Robomimic · MuJoCo · PyTorch

Modern Replay: Stable and High-Performance Sampling for Deep RL

Timeline: November 2025 - Present

Status: In progress

Current Output: D-SPEAR: Dual-Stream Prioritized Experience Adaptive Replay for Stable Reinforcement Learning in Robotic Manipulation

Modern Replay is a research project focused on improving experience replay mechanisms to achieve both training stability and high performance in deep reinforcement learning. It introduces structured and adaptive sampling strategies that decouple optimization dynamics, reduce gradient variance, and enable more stable and efficient policy learning.

Research Details

  • Proposes a modern replay framework that improves both stability and performance via advanced sampling strategies.
  • Introduces structured sampling mechanisms to balance different learning dynamics during training.
  • Reduces gradient variance, leading to more stable optimization and better convergence behavior.
  • Demonstrates consistent performance gains over standard replay methods on MuJoCo continuous control benchmarks.

Technologies: Deep Reinforcement Learning · Experience Replay · Sampling Optimization · MuJoCo Simulation
